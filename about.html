About

A research-first approach to understanding books and their readers.

I'm Alexander Grace Wright. I work with authors to understand how their books are positioned, who their readers are, and what realistic opportunities exist to connect the two.

Add alexander.png Upload your photo to this folder

I bring more than eight years of experience across marketing, research, promotion, and related work, including book marketing specifically. That broader background shapes how I approach every project. I've spent years learning how audiences form, how positioning influences discovery, and how research reduces guesswork in any promotional effort, and I apply that thinking directly to the specific challenges authors face.

Book marketing has its own particular dynamics. Readers discover titles through recommendation systems, category placement, genre expectations, and word of mouth that often follows patterns specific to publishing. Part of my work has involved studying those patterns closely, so that the strategies I recommend are grounded in how book discovery actually happens, not in generic marketing assumptions borrowed from unrelated industries.

I work with authors from every publishing background. Independently published authors, traditionally published authors, debut authors, and established authors with multiple titles all face a version of the same underlying question: how does a reader who would genuinely enjoy this book actually find it? My job is to help answer that question with research rather than assumption.

Why Research Comes First

Strategy without research is just a guess with better formatting.

It's tempting to jump straight into promotional activity: ads, social posts, review requests. But without understanding a book's positioning and its realistic reader base first, that activity is directionless. I've seen how much more effective marketing becomes once it's grounded in a clear picture of who the book is actually for.

That's why every engagement starts with careful observation of the book itself before any recommendation is made.

Observation before recommendation

I read your book's description, categories, genre signals, and catalogue before offering any opinion on strategy.

Patterns over assumptions

Reader behaviour in publishing follows patterns. Research helps distinguish what's likely to be relevant to your book from generic marketing advice.

Honesty about outcomes

Research informs better decisions, but it doesn't guarantee sales, reviews, or rankings. I won't promise outcomes that can't be controlled.

A Note on How I Work

I'd rather tell you what the research shows than what you want to hear.

Some books have clear, strong opportunities waiting to be acted on. Others require more patience, more targeted research, or a narrower focus. I aim to be straightforward about which situation applies to your book, and to build a plan around that honest assessment rather than a generic promise.
